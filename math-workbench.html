Understand the mathematics behind aquatic trajectories

Change the inputs and watch mathematical quantities update. These illustrations are deterministic, low-dimensional teaching examples—not a browser implementation of every v0.1.1 method or a camera calibration.

1. Timestamp-aware swimming speed

v = (p₂ − p₁)/Δt; speed = √(Δx²+Δy²)/Δt

Matches the finite-difference concept in aqt_kinematics(). The released function also requires valid observed positions, positive timestamps, and permitted time gaps.

Displacement vector

2. Group directional polarization

P = ‖(1/N) Σ [vᵢ / ‖vᵢ‖]‖

This visualization uses five nonzero unit headings, so polarization lies between 0 and 1. aqt_group_metrics() additionally requires simultaneous valid observations.

Five synthetic headings

3. Cross-camera affine clock relationship

t_reference = a + (1 + d · 10⁻⁶) × t_camera

The displayed mapping is algebraic. Fitted and holdout pulse residuals require independently corresponding physical synchronization events. See aqt_sync_holdout_fit().
