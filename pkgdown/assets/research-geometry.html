How two camera rays become a 3D coordinate

An interactive 3D *teaching* reconstruction: two idealized cameras above a planar air–water surface observe one known synthetic fish position below it. The browser solves Snell's law at a single interface, refracts perturbed incident rays and finds their closest-point midpoint. This is not a fitted underwater GoPro flat-port/glass camera calibration.

What is physically specified?

n_air sin(θ_air) = n_water sin(θ_water)   •   rᵢ(λ) = oᵢ + λ dᵢ   •   X̂ = (r₁(λ*) + r₂(μ*))/2

The transmitted ray uses the tangential Snell component and a negative vertical component. The estimator minimizes squared distance between the two transmitted rays and abstains when they are nearly parallel or intersect only behind the interface. The image perturbation is deterministic and illustrative, not a calibrated detector measurement-error distribution.

Limits: no thick glass, housing port, Brown lens distortion, real GoPro intrinsics, physical survey, detector model or real cross-camera identity matching. The synthetic XYZ error is calculated because the synthetic truth is known; it is not empirical underwater accuracy.
